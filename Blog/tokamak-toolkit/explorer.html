Integrated modelling for MAST-U and ITER

A tokamak confines a hot, electrically charged gas called plasma using magnetic fields. To calculate how that plasma changes, we need models for its magnetic shape, heating, transport of heat and particles, and edge conditions. Each model depends on quantities calculated by the others.

Choose a machine, establish the scenario assumptions, then follow a plasma update. Each module explains its role, inputs, outputs, governing relation and implementation limits. The evidence section separates numerical checks from physical validation.

An introduction to the plasma physics

MAST here refers to the MAST Upgrade (MAST-U) modelling route. The drawings are schematic. The MAST-U cases use simulated MAST-U-like geometry; the ITER cases are modelling benchmarks.

5. Read the evidence and its limits

Implementation status is not a validation verdict. Read each result with its recorded scenario, acceptance criterion and claim status.

What the different checks establish
Numerical convergence
Refining the time step tests sensitivity to the numerical protocol. It does not establish agreement with an experiment or remove model bias.
Reference comparison
Comparing profiles with a declared reference tests accuracy in that case. A failed or non-evaluable comparison cannot support a validation claim.
Formal local robustness
A proof bounds a stated property inside a specified input region. An unresolved proof is not a counterexample; an out-of-domain input is outside that contract.
