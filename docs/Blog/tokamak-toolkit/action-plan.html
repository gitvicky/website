JOREK: finish J5, then decide its role

  1. Done: the second case (b) job is analysed with the pre-registered window rule. n = 12, 16 and 20 grow at the pedestal, weakly; n = 1–8 show no measurable growth by the end (entry 73).
  2. Done: the time-step check. Each of n = 12, 16 and 20 restarted from the same snapshot at Δt = 1 and Δt = 0.5; halving the step changes γ by about one part in ten thousand (entry 75). The doubled grid’s axisymmetric state is ready.

  4. Report the case (b) growth rates per n with their convergence status, and add a ceiling if the grid check stays open.
  5. The owner then decides what JOREK becomes in the toolkit: a surrogate of a JOREK quantity, reference runs only, or neither. One case (b) n-scan costs about two six-hour node-jobs before any convergence check, which bears on that choice.

After M4: M5 inputs

M5 propagates uncertainty through the coupled simulation and validates it, ending in a physicist sign-off. Inputs to ask for at the M4 review: a Tier A validation set (experimental shot data are out of scope), the uncertain inputs and their priors, the quantity of interest, and how the discrepancy budget is split across transport, sources, pedestal and equilibrium. The certified boxes and conformal intervals from M4 become M5’s trust regions and surrogate-error terms.
