05. A stall explained, RF heating, and two stubborn builds

2026-09-22 · milestone M1

With several decisions waiting on the owner (the benchmark tolerance, the STEP data file, the FreeGSNKE machine description), this iteration took the unblocked items in turn.

1. Why did TORAX’s Newton solver give up around t = 55 s?

Post 03 left a loose end. In the ITER ramp-up, with time steps of 0.5 s or less, TORAX’s Newton solver reported “not converged” on a handful of steps between t = 54.5 s and 57 s. Smaller steps are normally easier to converge, so this needed explaining before anyone builds on it.

Was something happening in the plasma? We checked the plasma current, minimum safety factor, heating and ohmic power, density, and internal inductance across 46–64 s. All smooth. Stepping through the window one step at a time, the transport coefficients were smooth too, with nothing switching on or off. Only the three edge (pedestal) points sat at their minimum value, and they do so throughout the run. So no physics event.

What was the solver doing? The failing steps quit after only 3–5 iterations, far below the limit of 30. They quit because of the line search. When a Newton step makes the residual worse, the solver tries a shorter one, then shorter again, and gives up once the step is below 1% of the original. The neighbouring steps told the rest of the story: many only reached TORAX’s “coarse” tolerance. The residual was hovering just around the convergence threshold of 10⁻⁵, and at that level the line search stalls. Even compilation details change which steps pass: running the same steps one at a time, outside the compiled loop, moves the failures around.

Does it matter? Three experiments settled it:

change failed steps stored energy at 80 s
TORAX defaults 6 53.998 MJ
double the iteration limit 6 (no effect) 53.998 MJ
let the line search shorten the step to 0.1% (tolerance unchanged) 0 53.999 MJ

So the failures are real but harmless: two parts in 10⁵ on the final energy. The reference runs never see them because TORAX’s adaptive stepping would simply retry such a step with a smaller Δt, and our fixed-step loop deliberately doesn’t. We have not changed TORAX’s defaults. The brief says stalls are reported, not tuned away, so the question of whether fixed-step runs may relax the line-search floor has gone to the owner (OQ-5).

2. Analytic Gaussian RF deposition

M1 asks for an analytic Gaussian RF heating model. This will be the trusted fallback when an RF-deposition surrogate arrives at M3. TORAX already has one: its electron-cyclotron source deposits a Gaussian of chosen centre and width, normalised to the requested power, with optional current drive. Writing our own would only duplicate it. So tkit.physics.rf is a small typed wrapper around it (GaussianRF(p_total, rho_dep, width, current_drive_efficiency)), plus physics tests on the ITER hybrid scenario with 10 MW deposited at ρ = 0.4:

check result
absorbed power equals the request to 10⁻⁶
heating and driven current peak at ρ = 0.4 within one grid cell
electron temperature inside ρ = 0.45 rises everywhere; stored energy rises yes

The last row is the “monotonic response to heating” test the brief asks for, now on a real scenario rather than the toy model.

3. Aurora and ASCOT5 finally build

Two open-source codes had refused to install at M0. Aurora does impurity transport and radiation. ASCOT5 is a fast-particle orbit code for beam and fusion-alpha heating sources, which M1 needs.

Aurora failed because its packaging uses a setting that newer versions of its build tool reject. A two-line patch fixed that. Then came a nastier surprise: installing it silently downgraded numpy and xarray to versions that break TORAX and JAX. We restored the versions, confirmed Aurora’s Fortran core still runs under the newer numpy, and wrote the lesson into the environment notes: every future install uses a constraints file that pins numpy and xarray.

ASCOT5 needed three workarounds: - the cluster’s HDF5 compiler wrapper rejects a flag the ASCOT5 build passes, so we compiled with plain gcc and explicit paths; - this gcc is configured to offload OpenMP code to NVIDIA GPUs, which broke the final link, so we turned offloading off; - loading the gcc module quietly blocks the Python and CMake modules, so gcc is used by its full path.

It now runs. As a sanity check, its analytic ITER magnetic field gives 4.70 T at R = 7 m, exactly the 1/R fall-off from 5.3 T at the 6.2 m major radius. Both codes have smoke tests, and the recipes are in spec/ENVIRONMENT.md so the builds can be repeated.
