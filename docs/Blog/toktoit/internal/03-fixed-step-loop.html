03. Our loop drives TORAX, and the reference isn’t converged

2026-09-21 · milestone M1

From experiment to component

Post 02 ended with an experiment: TORAX’s single-step function runs inside our own fixed-step loop. That is now a proper module, tkit.physics.torax_loop. It does three things:

  • switches any TORAX scenario to a fixed time step;
  • runs it inside lax.scan with memory checkpointing at each step, as the brief requires;
  • records a compact trace at every step (temperatures, density, poloidal flux, stored energy) together with TORAX’s own convergence flag. Non-convergence is reported as a stall, just as in our Newton solver.

Three checks now guard it:

test what it shows
our loop vs TORAX’s own driver, same fixed-step config agree to 10⁻¹³
our loop vs the stored ITER ramp-up reference agree to 10⁻¹⁴ (see below for why this is possible)
gradient of stored energy through three coupled TORAX steps reverse mode matches finite differences to 10⁻⁶, forward and reverse agree to 10⁻⁹

Asking the obvious question: how small should the time step be?

With a fixed-step loop, the natural first experiment is to shrink the step and watch the answer converge. We compared each run against the stored reference, and got a surprise: the smaller the step, the further we landed from the reference, with core temperature off by 20–46%.

Our first guess was sawtooth crashes. These are discrete events, and their timing could depend on the step size. That was wrong: the ramp-up scenario has no sawtooth model at all. Looking at the error as a function of time told the real story.

ITER ramp-up, core electron temperature for three time steps:

time Δt = 1 s Δt = 0.5 s Δt = 0.25 s difference, 1 s vs 0.25 s
2 s 24.0 keV 27.4 keV 29.4 keV 18%
10 s 24.6 keV 24.0 keV 23.7 keV 3.7%
40 s 11.1 keV 11.0 keV 11.0 keV 1.0%
80 s 8.56 keV 8.54 keV 8.53 keV 0.4%

In the first few seconds the plasma heats up fast, and a coarse step cannot follow it. Later the differences shrink, and they do so in the pattern expected of a first-order time scheme: the gap between 1 s and 0.25 s is three times the gap between 0.5 s and 0.25 s. So our loop is behaving correctly. The issue is the reference itself: TORAX’s ramp-up example uses fixed 2 s steps, which still carries 1–4% time-discretisation error through the flat part of the discharge and much more early on. It is also why we could match the reference to 10⁻¹⁴: we simply used the same 2 s step.

ITER flat-top. This scenario uses TORAX’s adaptive time step, which is chosen from the size of the diffusivities. Here the finest fixed step we tried (12.5 ms) differs from the adaptive reference by up to 34% in core temperature at t ≈ 2 s, and by 5% at the end. The adaptive steps, up to 0.42 s long, are too coarse for the heating transient.

None of this is a TORAX bug. The examples are tuned to run fast, not to be time-converged. But it matters for what “reproduce the published benchmark within tolerance” means. If the published comparison was made with these settings, then part of any difference we see will be time-step error, not physics. That question has gone to the owner and the physicist reviewer (OQ-5), with a recommendation. First compare like with like, at the published settings. Then report the time-converged answer separately, as its own line in the error budget.

Something we haven’t explained yet

In the ramp-up with steps of 0.5 s or less, TORAX’s Newton solver reports non-convergence on a handful of steps after t ≈ 55 s. That is counterintuitive, since smaller steps are usually easier to converge. It is logged in the trace and flagged in OQ-5. We’ll dig into it when we connect our own transport model, since both concern the same nonlinear solve.

Housekeeping

The test suite has grown to 56 tests. Several run real TORAX simulations, so each test file now runs in its own process to stay under the login node’s 600 s CPU limit.
